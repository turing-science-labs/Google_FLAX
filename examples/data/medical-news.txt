"New weight-loss drug eases knee osteoarthritis pain in major trials": The knee is the joint most commonly affected by osteoarthritis, a condition in which the cartilage in joints wears away, causing pain, swelling, stiffness, and impaired movement. According to the World Health OrganizationTrusted Source (WHO), older age, joint injury, and overweight or obesity are the major risk factors for osteoarthritis. Studies suggest that two-thirds of people with obesity have knee osteoarthritis, and the incidence of knee osteoarthritis increases with increasing body mass index (BMI). Now, two phase 3 trials of retatrutide, published in The New England Journal of Medicine, have shown that the medication significantly improves pain symptoms in people with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Over 68 weeks, roughly two-thirds of participants treated with retatrutide lost at least 15% of their body weight and reported that their knee pain had been cut in half.

"Retatrutide acts on 3 receptors to aid weight loss": Retatrutide is the latest weekly injectable weight loss medication, following on from semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), a GLP-1 agonist, and tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), a GLP-1/GIP agonist. The medications were developed to control blood glucose levels in people with type 2 diabetes, but are increasingly prescribed for weight management as they help to decrease a person’s appetite. Retatrutide is a triple agonist, which acts on the GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors, and is currently in phase 3 clinical trials as a treatment for several conditions, including obesity, type 2 diabetes, knee osteoarthritis pain, and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). It has not yet been licensed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.

"Significant improvements in knee osteoarthritis pain": To investigate the medication’s effect on knee osteoarthritis, researchers recruited just over 1,000 people without type 2 diabetes, who had both obesity and knee osteoarthritis, in two phase 3 trials of weekly retatrutide injections. Both trials were funded by the manufacturer, Eli Lilly. In the first, TRIUMPH-4, 445 people were randomized to receive 9mg or 12mg (the maximum prescribed dose) of retatrutide, or placebo. In the second, TRIUMPH-1-OA, researchers randomized 574 participants to receive retatrutide at 4mg, 9mg, or 12mg, or placebo. TRIUMPH-4 ran for 68 weeks, and the primary endpoints were the change in WOMAC pain score — a 0 to 10 scale where the lower the score the better — and the change in weight. TRIUMPH-1-OA ran for 80 weeks, and one of its primary endpoints was the change in WOMAC pain score. In TRIUMPH-4, at 68 weeks, pain scores for people on retatrutide had decreased by 3.96 (9mg) and 3.74 (12mg), compared with a reduction of 2.12 with placebo. Those on retatrutide also lost around one-fifth of their body weight, whereas those on placebo lost, on average, just under 5%. In the TRIUMPH-1-OA trial, participants’ pain scores at week 80 had decreased by 3.22 (4 mg), 3.48 (9 mg), and 3.64 (12 mg), whereas people on placebo saw a reduction of 1.87.

"Description": Perindopril and amlodipine is a combination of medicines that are used to treat high blood pressure (hypertension). High blood pressure adds to the workload of the heart and arteries. If it continues for a long time, the heart and arteries may not function properly. This can damage the blood vessels of the brain, heart, and kidneys, resulting in a stroke, heart failure, or kidney failure. Lowering blood pressure can reduce the risk of strokes and heart attacks. Perindopril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor. It works by blocking a substance in the body that causes the blood vessels to tighten. This relaxes the blood vessels and lowers blood pressure. Amlodipine is a calcium channel blocker (CCB). It affects the movement of calcium into the cells of the heart and blood vessels. This relaxes the blood vessels and lowers blood pressure. A lower blood pressure will increase the supply of blood and oxygen to the heart.

"Proper Use": In addition to using this medicine, treatment for your high blood pressure may include weight control and changes in the types of foods you eat, especially foods high in sodium (salt). Your doctor will tell you which of these are most important for you. You should check with your doctor before changing your diet.
Remember that this medicine will not cure your high blood pressure, but it does help control it. You must continue to take it as directed if you expect to lower your blood pressure and keep it down. You might have to take high blood pressure medicine for the rest of your life. If high blood pressure is not treated, it can cause serious problems such as heart failure, blood vessel disease, strokes, or kidney disease. This medicine comes with a patient information insert. Read and follow these instructions carefully. Ask your doctor if you have any questions. You may take this medicine with or without food.
