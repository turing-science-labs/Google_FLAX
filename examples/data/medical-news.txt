"New weight-loss drug eases knee osteoarthritis pain in major trials"

The knee is the joint most commonly affected by osteoarthritis, a condition in which the cartilage in joints wears away, causing pain, swelling, stiffness, and impaired movement. According to the World Health OrganizationTrusted Source (WHO), older age, joint injury, and overweight or obesity are the major risk factors for osteoarthritis.
Studies suggest that two-thirds of people with obesity have knee osteoarthritis, and the incidence of knee osteoarthritis increases with increasing body mass index (BMI).
Now, two phase 3 trials of retatrutide, published in The New England Journal of Medicine, have shown that the medication significantly improves pain symptoms in people with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Over 68 weeks, roughly two-thirds of participants treated with retatrutide lost at least 15% of their body weight and reported that their knee pain had been cut in half.

"Retatrutide acts on 3 receptors to aid weight loss"

Retatrutide is the latest weekly injectable weight loss medication, following on from semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), a GLP-1 agonist, and tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), a GLP-1/GIP agonist. The medications were developed to control blood glucose levels in people with type 2 diabetes, but are increasingly prescribed for weight management as they help to decrease a person’s appetite. Retatrutide is a triple agonist, which acts on the GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors, and is currently in phase 3 clinical trials as a treatment for several conditions, including obesity, type 2 diabetes, knee osteoarthritis pain, and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). It has not yet been licensed by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
